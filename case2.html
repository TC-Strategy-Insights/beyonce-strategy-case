Executive Summary

01. The Context

The Question: Airbnb is widely described as costing about half what a hotel does. If that is accurate, hotels face real pressure to cut prices. The question is whether a citywide average is a fair way to measure it.

bolt

02. The Action

The Analysis: Pulled 30,259 real NYC Airbnb listings and cleaned them in Python, fixing price formats, checking for missing data, and setting aside a small number of extreme listings that would distort the average. Then compared the results against official NYC hotel rates.

ads_click

03. The Result

The Finding: The real difference is $93, not the $159 most comparisons report. Hotels average $334 a night, and a typical Manhattan Airbnb is $241. Narrow it further to entire homes in Manhattan, the product a hotel actually competes with, and the gap falls to $72. The larger figure appears only when you average all five boroughs together and count private rooms ($103) alongside entire homes ($223).
What to do about it: Hotels should not panic and cut prices to compete. The $159 figure overstates the pressure. Compare against listings in your own market, and price against that.

Why this matters:
The $159 figure averages two different things at once. It mixes every borough together, and it mixes private rooms in with entire homes. A private room typically costs $103 and an entire home $223, and only the entire home competes with a hotel room. Match the borough and the difference drops to $93. Match the listing type as well and it falls to $72. A hotel that discounts against the $159 figure is responding to more competitive pressure than it actually faces.

⚠ One important catch:
These two numbers are not measured the same way. The hotel figure counts rooms people actually paid for. The Airbnb figure is the price hosts are asking, which includes listings that may not book. The true difference is likely smaller than $93. This is a useful benchmark, not an exact comparison.

Why hotels can charge more

NYC hotels were 84.1% full in 2025, the highest occupancy in the country for three years running, while charging a record $334 a night. Demand that strong does not call for a price cut.

What the law changed

In 2023, Local Law 18 placed strict rules on short-term rentals in NYC, and the number of listings dropped afterward. Fewer listings means less pressure on hotel pricing, which helps explain why hotel rates have stayed high.

The bottom line:
Hotels do cost more. They are nearly full, and regulation has limited how many short-term rentals operate. But how much more depends entirely on the comparison. Citywide it looks like $159. Against comparable Manhattan listings it is $93. Pricing off the first number overstates the pressure hotels are actually under.
